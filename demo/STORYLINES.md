# Storylines

Three different films for the AlreadyCovered demo. Recommendation: **A**.

## A — "The 6am text" (recommended)

**Device:** a moment in someone's day. **Form:** object-to-object chain (the
phone becomes the laptop screen becomes the app).

A teacher's phone buzzes at 6:02am with a text: she's sick today. Cut to her
laptop, already open to AlreadyCovered — because she set it up last week.
The camera walks through the real, live product: her uploaded lesson files,
the schedule, the AI-drafted substitute instructions grounded in her actual
worksheet, her one-click review, and the real exported PDF. It ends on that
PDF, genuinely opened, scrolled, readable. The viewer should feel: "this is
a real tool solving a real 6am problem," not watching a feature tour.

- First two seconds: a phone screen, dark bedroom, a text notification
  slides in reading "Can't come in today - not feeling well." Time stamp
  6:02 AM.
- Beats: hook (the text) → tension (what she'd normally have to do, named in
  one line, not acted out) → reveal (AlreadyCovered title) → proof (upload →
  auto-matched material → AI draft → review → approve) → payoff (the real
  PDF, scrolled) → claim → end card.
- Look: warm off-white ground (#faf8f4), dark ink (#201e1a) text, teal
  accent (#0f6e63) on the one verified state per shot, Inter throughout —
  all pulled directly from the live site's own tokens, not invented.
- Sound: a soft single notification chime on the text; a light UI click per
  action beat; a warm, unhurried instrumental bed (no lyrics) under the
  whole film, ducking under the chime and clicks.

## B — "Before / after, split screen"

**Device:** life before and after. **Form:** split screen to one.

Left half: a teacher at 11pm, kitchen table covered in papers, visibly
tired, typing into a blank document (never a real distressed photo of a
real identifiable person — a generic, respectfully-framed illustrative
scene). Right half, same moment, calm: a teacher who already has
AlreadyCovered open, reviewing an already-drafted packet. The two panes
collapse into one as the right side "wins," then the film continues in the
real product the same way as storyline A. Riskier: needs generic stock-style
"before" footage I'd have to source or stage, which is more production
overhead and less grounded in real material than A.

## C — "The result first"

**Device:** the result first. **Form:** an endless zoom / pull-back.

Opens tight on the exported PDF already in a substitute's hands, mid-read,
normal pace. The camera pulls back through the PDF viewer, through the
export screen, back through review, back through upload — rewinding the
whole chain in reverse to reveal where it came from, then plays forward
once more into the real end card. Visually the boldest of the three (a
genuine "wait, how did we get here" hook), but asks more of the reconstructed
UI transitions holding up to a reverse pull-back than A does, which is why
it isn't the recommendation given the real time available.
