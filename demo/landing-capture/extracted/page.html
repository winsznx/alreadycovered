The problem

Sub plans get assembled at the worst possible moment.

Most sub-plan tools still need you to gather context and assemble resources once an absence is already happening. AlreadyCovered keeps the draft current in advance, so there's less to do when you're least able to do it.

Before

Why it works this way

Grounded by construction, not by hope

Every claim traces to a source

The model is only ever shown excerpts from your own indexed files and can only cite IDs it was actually given. Anything it cites outside that set is stripped before you ever see it.

Missing means flagged, not filled in

No worksheet, no safety procedure, and no lesson date is ever invented. If the material isn't there, you see an honest "needs attention" instead of a confident-sounding guess.

Your corrections stick

Edit a step, reject a match, or confirm a routine - your changes are preserved across regeneration, and a reviewed packet is only ever "reviewed" for the exact version you approved.

Questions

Good questions, honest answers

Who can see my files?+

Only you. Your uploads and generated packets are private to your account, stored separately from every other teacher's. Nothing is sold to advertisers, used to train external models, or shared with your school automatically - and the app never collects student-roster or grade-book data.

What file types are supported?+

PDF (with a real text layer), DOCX, TXT, and Markdown. A scanned or image-only PDF is flagged as “no readable text” rather than guessed at.

What happens if a worksheet is missing?+

The lesson is marked “needs attention” with a plain-language reason. The app never invents a substitute worksheet or a page number - you link the right file or leave it clearly noted as missing in the export.

Can I edit what the AI drafted?+

Yes. Every instruction is editable, and your edit is preserved - re-generating a stale lesson after a file change never silently overwrites your correction.
